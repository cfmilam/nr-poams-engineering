# Generation-1 instrument and containment envelope

## Verdict

**PARTIAL:** sensitivity and retained-energy envelopes overlap in principle. Only an inert loaded-stage instrument prototype is presently justified.

## Architecture

Use a counterbalanced flexure/null balance with optical error readout, electrostatic null actuation and a dissimilar second force reference. Put the complete rotor, motor/stator reaction structure, bearings, inner containment and local sensors on the measured side. Prefer onboard power/optical telemetry or a qualified coast window; separately inject and measure every service crossing.

A facility-fixed outer guard surrounds the moving pod with clearance. Guard contact or aerodynamic coupling invalidates the measurement window.

## Prototype acceptance

With a nonspinning full-mass dummy pod:

1. operate at null for the planned dwell;
2. blindly recover 10, 40, 100 and 400 nN vertical injections;
3. demonstrate `u(D) <= 8 nN`;
4. bound injected tilt, torque, vibration, thermal and magnetic transfer;
5. make two dissimilar force references agree within uncertainty;
6. repeat after remount and 180-degree apparatus reversal.

Green performance opens an independently engineered 100 J study, beginning at much lower energy. Amber performance (`8–40 nN`) calls for instrument improvement, not automatic energy escalation. Red performance (`>40 nN` or unstable/contact-dependent transfer) stops the route.

## Independent safety gate

Before any spin, qualified mechanical review must close rotor stress/fatigue, critical speed, hub/shaft/bearing/fastener failures, independent overspeed protection, credible maximum energy, fragment trajectories including axial escape, validated inner containment, fixed outer guard, remote operation, pressure-vessel obligations, safe rundown, interlocks, exclusion zone and emergency procedures.

Ideal thin-ring stress is not containment design. The force balance may be sacrificial; personnel protection may not.
