The Fine-Structure Ratio as a Closure Eigenvalue of Angular Momentum

A derivation within Normal Realism / the Pope–Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis (POAMS)

Working paper — the derivation is forced to a single remaining object; the final numerical value is not yet closed. Every step below is derived before comparison with measurement; no step is fitted to the observed value.

Abstract. We show that within POAMS the quantity conventionally packaged as the fine-structure constant $\alpha$ is not a fundamental input but the eigenvalue of a self-closure condition on the one substance (angular momentum). Reasoning forward from the established POAMS results — the derivation of the spectrum from the Pythagorean time relation, and the geometric account of fine-structure splitting — we obtain, with no free parameters: (i) that $\alpha$ is a pure number, forced by the intransitivity of quantum transactions; (ii) that $\alpha \neq 0$ is a topological necessity (the hairy-ball theorem on the observer sphere); (iii) that $\alpha$ is small because the closure kernel is exactly marginal; (iv) that $\alpha$ is transcendental; and (v) the closed relation $\;\alpha \sim (4\pi/W)^{1/4}$, where $W$ is the weight of the Machian closure over the observer sphere. The closure geometry is forced to be the tetrahedral four-cap complex (the cuboctahedron). What remains open is the identity of $W$ — every finite invariant of that complex is shown to miss the required magnitude, cornering $W$ into a single continuum object (a $\zeta$-regularized spectral determinant), which is the subject of ongoing work. The value $\alpha$ owes the world is a measured line-splitting ratio; POAMS owes that ratio, and it does not owe the fantasy package $e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c$.

1. What must be derived, and what must not

The conventional fine-structure constant is defined as $\alpha = e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c$: a ratio built from electric charge $e$, the permittivity of empty space $\varepsilon_0$, and $c$ read as a speed. In Normal Realism none of these three is a physical entity. There is no charge — what is called "charge sign" is the sign of intrinsic spin angular momentum, cashed out in joules of spin kinetic energy [1]. There is no void with a permittivity — "space" is angular-momentum relations, not a container. And $c$ is a conversion factor between length- and time-measures, not a velocity [1]. So $\alpha$ as conventionally packaged is a ratio of non-entities, and POAMS is not obliged to reproduce that package.

What the laboratory actually measures is a physical fact: a small dimensionless ratio between the split components of a spectral line. POAMS owes an account of that ratio. This paper derives its structure.

GROUND Two POAMS results are used as established input and are not re-derived here:
  1. The spectrum is derived from first principles. From the Pythagorean time-dilation relation $t_R^2 = (s/c)^2 + t^2$ [1, App. 1], taking the ratio of resultant to subject time as discrete, $t_R/t = n/N$, and substituting $e = mv^2$, one obtains $e = mc^2(1 - N^2/n^2)$ and hence $f = cR\,(1 - N^2/n^2)$ — the Balmer–Rydberg law — with scale $cR = mc^2/h$ [1, App. 3]. Balmer and Rydberg only fitted this; POAMS derives it.
  2. The fine-structure splitting mechanism is geometric. Lines split through the coupling of a mode's angular harmonic $l$ with its phase sense (leading/lagging spin); different $(l,\text{phase})$ combinations sit at slightly different energies. No charge, no field, no external spin–orbit force.

2. The ratio is a tilt on the time-cone

In the observer's Pythagorean time-cone, a vortex's internal circulation contributes a leg $s/c$ to the resultant time $t_R$, tilted by an angle $\theta$ off the proper-time axis:

$$\sin\theta = \frac{s/c}{t_R} = \frac{v}{c}.$$

Here $v/c$ is not a speed ratio but the sine of the cone's opening angle — the fraction of a vortex's circulation that projects into the observer's spatial measure. The measured fine-structure splitting scales as $(v/c)^2$; the ratio we must derive is $\sin\theta$ itself.

NO-GO The isolated cone cannot fix $\theta$. The relation $t_R^2=(s/c)^2+t^2$ is scale-invariant: it fixes only the similarity class of the triangle, and $\tan\theta$ is the free parameter of that family. Winding closure $\oint d\varphi = 2\pi n$ fixes only the integer $n$, which lives in the phase plane, orthogonal to the tilt. The isolated inventory $\{c,\hbar,n,2\pi\}$ contains but two dimensionful quantities, so no dimensionless ratio can be formed from it. A single vortex on an empty cone therefore has no forced tilt. The ratio must come from a relation of the substance to itself.

3. The Machian closure and its forced kernel

The vortex is not isolated. It is a standing pattern in the one temporal substance shared by all vortices — the Machian totality. Its tilt must be self-consistent: the tilt it must adopt to remain stationary in the background equals the tilt that the background, itself composed of such vortices, imposes. This is a fixed-point condition $\sin\theta = F(\sin\theta)$.

Pope's transactional ontology forces the form of $F$ [1, ch. 10]. Transactions are (i) double-ended — each a complete source–sink pair, a $2\pi$ winding; (ii) reciprocal — equal and opposite (Law of Moments / Newton's third law), one conserved $h$-unit, no remainder; (iii) intransitive — $A\!\leftrightarrow\!B$ and $B\!\leftrightarrow\!C$ do not compose to $A\!\leftrightarrow\!C$ [1, §10.9]. A naïve reciprocal balance is homogeneous of degree one — marginal — reproducing the scale-invariance as a criticality:

THEOREM Machian criticality. The reciprocal transactional gain is exactly $\kappa = 1$: the totality is self-sustaining, at its own critical point. The forced kernel is $$G(\theta) = \cos\theta\,(2-\cos\theta) = 1 - (1-\cos\theta)^2,$$ which has a double root at $\theta = 0$. A standing tilt exists only if the totality's global closure fails marginality by a $\theta$-independent amount $\delta$: $$(1-\cos\theta)^2 = \delta \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{v}{c} = \sin\theta = \sqrt{2\sqrt{\delta}-\delta}\;\approx\;\sqrt{2}\,\delta^{1/4}.$$

Two consequences are immediate and forced. First, the smallness of $v/c$ is not an accident: it is the signature of the totality sitting near its own criticality, a quartic-stiffness zero mode ($\theta^4$). Second, the whole problem reduces to one $\theta$-independent number $\delta$.

4. Why $\delta$ is a pure, nonzero, transcendental number

FORCED $\delta$ is a pure number (hence $\alpha$ is). Intransitivity forbids the self-return from chaining through the totality; the return is confined to a single closed two-party transaction. The coupling therefore carries no particle count $N$, no distance, and no epoch — it is dimensionless and finite. The Standard Model does not explain why $\alpha$ is a pure number; here it follows from the sonship (intransitivity) argument.

FORCED $\delta \neq 0$ is a topological necessity. A standing circulation is a tangent structure on the observer sphere $S^2$. Since $\chi(S^2)=2\neq 0$, the hairy-ball / Poincaré–Hopf theorem [3] forbids a defect-free closure: one cannot comb the sphere flat. Independently, the only exact tiling of the $4\pi$ frame by $2\pi$ caps is two hemispheres, whose union along the shared equator is precisely the forbidden chaining. So $\alpha\neq 0$ is required by topology — not a contingent fact.

FORCED $\delta$ is transcendental. The total closure defect of the frame is pinned by Gauss–Bonnet at $2\pi\chi(S^2)=4\pi$ [3], so the closure weight enters as $\delta = 4\pi/W$. A sub-leading, non-topologically-protected defect over the curved frame is generically transcendental; equivalently, a multiplicative (non-chaining) closure yields $e^{-A}$ with $A$ built from $\pi$-quantities, transcendental by Lindemann–Weierstrass [4]. This forecloses any clean closed-form ("$\pi$-monomial") value — and is precisely why historical geometric formulas for $\alpha$ fail (see §7).

5. The forced closure geometry: the tetrahedral four-cap complex

What web of intransitive double-ended caps closes the $4\pi$ sphere? A double-ended link is an open cap (closed hemispheres share a full equatorial arc and would chain). A point $x\in S^2$ is covered by the cap with pole $p$ iff $x\cdot p > 0$; the caps cover $S^2$ iff the origin lies in the interior of the convex hull of the poles. By Carathéodory's theorem in $\mathbb{R}^3$ [5] this requires at least four poles, in tetrahedral configuration (pole–pole angle $\arccos(-\tfrac13)=109.47^\circ$).

FORCED The minimal intransitive closure of $S^2$ is the tetrahedral four-cap complex. The four boundary great circles in general position form a cell complex with $$(V,E,F) = (12,\,24,\,14), \qquad \chi = 12-24+14 = 2,$$ which is precisely the cuboctahedron (12 vertices, 24 edges, 14 faces = 8 triangles + 6 squares). This is the forced geometric arena in which $W$ lives.

6. The relation to the totality, correctly framed

In Normal Realism the universe is constant — neither expanding nor contracting — and has no age (there is no external clock by which to measure one). What conventional cosmology reads as "recession" is the angular-momentum-conservation velocity of deep-field objects: a deeper line of sight encloses more mass, sits nearer the distributed barycentre, and by conservation of angular momentum registers a higher velocity. The totality is, moreover, a holographic projection from the observer pole [1, ch. 4]. Consequently $W$ is to be derived in here, from the closure/projection geometry, and not measured out there.

This is a strong constraint, and a clarifying one: because the universe is constant and $W$ is a fixed property of the projection, $\alpha$ is not a time-varying quantity. Any predicted drift of $\alpha$ with cosmic epoch would signal that a container-cosmology assumption had entered; none does.
