1. The one formula

Let $t$ be an atom's intrinsic (proper) spectral period, $t_R$ its period as registered by an observer, and $s$ their separation. Since $s/c$ is a duration, the three measures compose orthogonally, and Pythagoras' theorem gives (Pope, App. 1):

$$t_R^2 = \left(\tfrac{s}{c}\right)^2 + t^2.$$

Setting $s=v\,t_R$ and simplifying returns the time-dilation relation — from geometry alone, with $c$ a units-conversion constant between length and time, not a velocity:

$$t_R=\frac{t}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.$$

This is the whole of the physics that follows. Drag the tilt below: the observer's registered time $t_R$ is the hypotenuse; as the motion-measure $s/c$ grows, $t_R$ stretches. There is no "moving light" here — only the geometry of proper and relative time.

2. First level: the spectrum, from discrete time

The one physical premise of Normal Realism: time is a discretum. So $t_R$ and $t$ take only discrete values, and their ratio is rational, $t_R/t=n/N$ for integers $n,N$ (Pope, App. 3). Substituting into the time formula and using $e=mv^2$ gives, by algebra alone,

$$\frac{n^2}{N^2}=\frac{1}{1-v^2/c^2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e=mc^2\!\left(1-\frac{N^2}{n^2}\right).$$

Dividing by Planck's constant $h$ turns energy into frequency, with $mc^2/h\equiv cR$ the Rydberg limit — and this is the Balmer–Rydberg formula:

$$f = N^2 cR\!\left(\frac{1}{n_1^2}-\frac{1}{n_2^2}\right).$$
