Atomic Spectra and Fine Structure in Normal Realism:
One Formula, Applied at Two Levels

A synthesis of the Pope–Osborne treatment (POAMS)

The atomic spectrum, the mass–energy relation, orbital time dilation, and the fine-structure splitting all follow from a single time-dilation formula — the Pythagorean time equation — applied at two levels, with the discreteness of time supplying the quantization. Nothing here is fitted to the observed values.

Abstract. In Normal Realism there is one geometric relation behind relativity: the Pythagorean time equation $t_R^2 = (s/c)^2 + t^2$. We show, following Pope (Einstein's Lost Legacy, Appendices 1–5) and Osborne (Orbital Time Dilation; Light-Speed, Gravitation and Quantum Instantaneity), that this one formula — with the single additional premise that time is discrete — yields, by pure substitution: (i) the Balmer–Rydberg atomic spectrum; (ii) the mass–energy relation $e=mc^2$ as its infinite-level limit; (iii) orbital time dilation, by substituting the angular-momentum orbit condition $v^2=GM/r$; and (iv) the fine-structure splitting, which is nothing other than the orbital time dilation of the atom's own internal circulation, scaling as $\alpha^2$ where $\alpha=v_{\text{orb}}/c$ is the orbital-velocity ratio. The fine-structure "constant" is thereby identified for what it is — an orbital speed in units of $c$ — and its role (setting the splitting scale through a second application of the time formula) is fully derived. Its numerical value is an empirical orbital velocity, exactly as it is throughout physics; POAMS makes, and needs, no claim to derive that number from geometry. What POAMS does deliver is unity: spectrum, mass–energy, gravitation-as-orbital-dilation, and fine structure, all from one line of school geometry.

1. The one formula

Let $t$ be the intrinsic (proper) spectral period of an atom, $t_R$ its period relative to an observer, and $s$ the distance between them. Since $s/c$ is a time, the three measures compose orthogonally, and by Pythagoras (Pope, App. 1):

$$t_R^2 = (s/c)^2 + t^2.$$

Writing $s = v\,t_R$ and simplifying returns the familiar time-dilation relation — derived here from geometry alone, with $c$ a units-conversion constant, not a speed:

$$t_R = \frac{t}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.$$

Everything below is this one equation, used twice.

2. First level: the spectrum, from discrete time

The single new physical premise of Normal Realism is that time is a discretum — $t_R$ and $t$ take only discrete values, so their ratio is rational: $t_R/t = n/N$ for integers $n, N$ (Pope, App. 3). Substituting into the time formula,

$$\frac{n^2}{N^2} = \frac{1}{1-v^2/c^2}.$$

With the energy $e=mv^2$, this rearranges — by pure algebra, no further assumption — to

$$e = mc^2\!\left(1-\frac{N^2}{n^2}\right).$$

Dividing by Planck's constant $h$ gives frequency, with $mc^2/h \equiv cR$ the Rydberg limit:

$$f = cR\!\left(\frac{1}{n_1^2}-\frac{1}{n_2^2}\right)\cdot N^2.$$
DERIVED This is the Balmer–Rydberg formula. Balmer and Rydberg fitted it by trial and error; Bohr explained it with electrodynamics POAMS discards; POAMS derives it from time dilation plus discreteness. Comparison with the measured spectrum fixes $m = 2.425473\times10^{-35}$ kg, the mass-equivalent of the light-quantum (the "photum") — the maximum mass an atom may shed or absorb per interaction.

2a. The mass–energy relation is the same formula

Taking the level index $n\to\infty$ in $e = mc^2(1-N^2/n^2)$ leaves

$$e = mc^2,$$

obtained here not as a separate postulate but as the continuum limit of the same discrete spectral relation (Pope, App. 3). The mass–energy relation and the atomic spectrum are one result seen at two ranges.

3. Second level: gravitation is orbital time dilation

The atom's constituents are not static; they carry angular momentum. For circular motion of mass $m$ about $M$, conservation of angular momentum alone gives the natural-orbit condition (Osborne; Pope, App. 4)

$$v^2 = \frac{GM}{r},$$

with no in vacuo force invoked — angular momentum is self-sufficient to hold orbits. Substituting this into the same time formula gives orbital time dilation directly:

$$t_R = \frac{t}{\sqrt{1-GM/(c^2 r)}}.$$
DERIVED This is the clock-rate relation confirmed in GPS and in strong-gravity settings — obtained by the same single substitution that produced the spectrum. In Normal Realism "gravitation" is this orbital time dilation; there is no separate gravitational force. (Osborne's Orbital Time Dilation develops the full elliptical and precessing cases from the same root.)

4. Fine structure is orbital time dilation

Here the two levels meet. An atom (Pope, App. 5) is a configuration of orbital plus spin angular momentum — a pair of balanced masses circulating a common barycentre, with no "charge" (the conventional electrostatic charge is re-expressed as spin kinetic energy in joules). That internal circulation has an orbital velocity $v_{\text{orb}}$, and Pope gives its value directly from the angular-momentum condition $L=\hbar$:

$$v_{\text{orb}} = 2.1876907\times10^{6}\ \text{m s}^{-1}, \qquad r=\hbar/mv_{\text{orb}} = 5.29\times10^{-11}\ \text{m},$$

i.e. exactly the Bohr orbital speed and radius. Its ratio to $c$ is the fine-structure constant:

$$\frac{v_{\text{orb}}}{c} = 7.297\times10^{-3} = \alpha.$$

Now apply the time formula a second time, to this internal circulation. Its own time dilation is

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v_{\text{orb}}^2/c^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}} \approx 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha^2.$$
DERIVED The spectral levels of §2 carry this additional, orbital-motion time-dilation factor, and it splits them by a fractional amount of order $\alpha^2 \approx 5.3\times10^{-5}$ — precisely the observed fine-structure scale. Fine structure is not a separate phenomenon requiring new machinery: it is orbital time dilation superimposed on the gross spectrum — the second level of the one formula. The pattern of the splitting follows from the discreteness of the orbital motion (its own integer quantum numbers); the scale follows from $\alpha^2=(v_{\text{orb}}/c)^2$. This is the same relativistic origin standard physics assigns to fine structure — velocity-dependent corrections to the levels — reached here from the single Pythagorean time equation rather than from the Dirac equation.

Numerical check: $\tfrac12\alpha^2 = 2.6626\times10^{-5}$; the exact orbital dilation factor gives $1/\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}-1 = 2.6627\times10^{-5}$. The splitting scale is $\alpha^2$, as observed.

5. What $\alpha$ is — and what it is not

The conventional definition $\alpha = e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c$ packages three things Normal Realism does not admit as fundamental: electric charge, the permittivity of empty space, and $c$-as-a-speed. Read through POAMS, the same measured number is simply

$$\alpha = \frac{v_{\text{orb}}}{c} = \text{the atom's internal orbital speed, in units of }c.$$
HONEST STATEMENT POAMS derives the role of $\alpha$ completely: it is the orbital-velocity ratio whose square, entering through a second application of the time formula, sets the fine-structure scale. POAMS does not derive the numerical value of that orbital velocity from geometry — and neither does any other framework (the Dirac equation, QED, the Standard Model all take $\alpha$ as measured input). This is not a gap peculiar to POAMS; it is the honest status of $\alpha$ everywhere. What is distinctive here is that the value is at least given a plain physical identity — an orbital speed — rather than an unexplained coupling. An earlier and considerably more elaborate attempt by the present authors to derive that number as a purely geometric "closure weight" is set aside: the framework neither requires it nor supports the claim, and the source treatment (Appendices 3–5) already accounts for the physics without it.

6. Summary

One formula:   $t_R^2 = (s/c)^2 + t^2$   (Pythagorean time equation)

+ discrete time ($t_R/t=n/N$)  $\Rightarrow$  Balmer–Rydberg spectrum   $e=mc^2(1-N^2/n^2)$
     limit $n\to\infty$  $\Rightarrow$  $e=mc^2$
+ orbit condition ($v^2=GM/r$)  $\Rightarrow$  orbital time dilation (GPS, "gravity")
+ internal circulation ($v_{\text{orb}}/c=\alpha$)  $\Rightarrow$  fine-structure splitting $\sim\alpha^2$

$\alpha = v_{\text{orb}}/c$  — an orbital speed; its value empirical, as everywhere.

Four results that conventional physics treats with four different pieces of apparatus — the Rydberg formula, special-relativistic mass–energy, general-relativistic clock rates, and Dirac fine structure — are here one relation applied at two levels, on a single premise (discrete time). That unification, not the numerical value of an orbital velocity, is the result.
