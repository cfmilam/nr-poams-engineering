Stanford Research Compatibility Map

A capability-led map of the researchers, laboratories, facilities, and institutional routes most likely to move Normal Realism and POAMS from bounded experiments to independently measured effects and working prototypes.

Executive judgment

Buy capabilities against experiments—not agreement with a worldview.

Stanford can materially accelerate the program through magnetic-material fabrication, spintronic device engineering, nanofabrication, precision metrology, experimental design, and prototype translation. Researchers do not need to accept POAMS ontology to do decisive work. They need a fair test, a complete nuisance model, preserved adverse results, and access to the raw record.

The strongest first scientific nucleus: Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D. as institutional bridge; Yuri Suzuki for magnetic-material experiments; Shan X. Wang for sensors and prototypes; nano@stanford for direct facility access; an independent statistician; and a metrologist introduced only after the apparatus is mature.

The research contract

Five gates for every engagement

The invitation is an instrument-and-null problem. It is never “prove POAMS.”

1 · Native intervention

Name the circulation, winding, phase, twist/writhe partition, or closure condition being changed.

2 · Raw observable

Name the voltage, displacement, torque, support record, phase, spectrum, image, or count before interpretation.

3 · Frozen discriminator

Fix the sign, ordering, reversal, threshold, scaling law, or null before the run.

4 · Direct controls

Measure thermal, magnetic, electrical, acoustic, mechanical, timing, and analyst transfer paths.

5 · Technological leverage

State what reliable control of the measured occurrence would permit us to build.

Standing exclusions

  • No theory-only sponsorship without a bounded theorem or apparatus decision.
  • No UAP framing in first technical contact.
  • No advanced claim before its lower-stage gate passes.
Institutional bridge

Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D. and the Sol Foundation

Openness is inferred only from public professional behavior—not from presumed private belief.

Why Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D. is unusually useful

Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D.’s biomedical subject matter is not the natural home for the first physics experiments. His value is architectural: he has built high-dimensional measurement platforms, collaborated across statistics and engineering, translated instruments into companies, and worked where evidence is difficult and reputationally contested.

Best role: translate two experiments into Stanford-legible scopes; recruit the experimental PI, device builder, and independent critic; govern preregistration, custody, blinding, and adverse-result preservation.

What Sol demonstrates

Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D. and Peter Skafish built a multidisciplinary institution around anomalous evidence, policy, public education, and research standards. That is evidence of convening ability and methodological aperture—not evidence that Sol or Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D. endorses NR/POAMS.

Boundary: do not make Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D. nominal PI outside his laboratory’s competence, and do not use Sol as the scientific premise of a Stanford proposal.

Priority 2 · independent metrology

Priority 2 · magnetic readout

Kathryn Moler

Mesoscopic magnetic imaging · quantum materials · superconducting devices

A strong independent readout critic once a magnetic or retained-state device has a frozen geometry, field scale, and expected signature.

Boundary: use to image a declared state—not to search broadly for an anomaly.

Priority 2 · conceptual audit

Trevor Teitel

Philosophy of science · ontology · laws · space and time

Best used to test whether an experimental claim actually follows from its measurements, and whether POAMS terms remain stable across model, apparatus, and interpretation.

Experiment teams

Match people to concrete experimental needs

Each campaign names the first objective rather than a hoped-for interpretation.

A

Magnetic alignment and driven phase response

Team: Suzuki · nano@stanford/Moore · Wang · independent statistician · Moler only after the signature is frozen.

First objective: separate static alignment from driven phase advance while measuring thermal, magnetic-gradient, acoustic, cable-force, and support transfer.

B

Persistent winding memory

Team: Wang · Suzuki · Mabuchi or Safavi-Naeini · nano@stanford.

First objective: demonstrate a retained, reversible, addressable state whose readout survives declared controls and whose transition rule was frozen in advance.

C

Mechanically ordered angular momentum

Team: RMC build · Digonnet controls · independent statistician · Kasevich late.

First objective: close ordinary support, vibration, magnetic, thermal, and aerodynamic channels before any anomalous inertial interpretation.

D

Rate-of-time material state

Team: Suzuki or Fisher · Lev or Dionne · Kasevich only for an earned consequence.

First objective: establish a directly measured, reversible material-state signature before attaching a rate interpretation.

E

Receiver and three-route optical work

Team: Ginzton/SPRC; Miller, Vučković, Fejer, Safavi-Naeini, or Mabuchi selected by the exact measurement requirement.

Sequence: Receiver Stages 0 and 1 must physically pass before Stage 2 or any capacity language.

Infrastructure

Facilities that change the timetable

Direct external access matters because it reduces dependence on a single faculty sponsor.

nano@stanford + shared SPM

External academic, government, private, and industrial organizations may open accounts, train users, and perform prototype R&D. Shared AFM/SPM platforms add topographic, electrical, magnetic, nanomechanical, thermal, Raman, and scanning-tunneling modes for independent device-state characterization.

Ginzton + Stanford Photonics

A cross-department network for mature optical and receiver questions. Enter with a specified wavelength, bandwidth, state preparation, raw detector record, and rejection controls—not a broad request to explore.

Adjacent RMC dry lab

Use for RMC-controlled preregistration, data custody, apparatus integration, nonhazardous bench work, consultant reviews, and prototype iteration. Proximity should lower friction while keeping equipment, people, data, and inventions visibly separate.

Institutional architecture

Keep three engagement rails separate

Money, access, staffing, reports, and intellectual-property rights travel differently on each rail.

1 · External facility use

RMC opens its own nano@stanford account, trains named users, and buys instrument time. This is the fastest route to practical familiarity and does not require a faculty research agreement.

2 · Sponsored research

Use a Stanford Sponsored Research Agreement when RMC funds named Stanford researchers for defined work and expects detailed reports or negotiated IP rights. One PI, one bounded apparatus question, milestone and stop gates.

3 · Personal consulting

Use for short design reviews, criticism, and strategy. Consulting does not rent Stanford laboratories, employees, or equipment. Keep personal advice and institutional work contractually distinct.

Execution

A disciplined first 90 days

Start small enough that a null is useful and a positive result can be independently repeated.

Days 1–30

Assemble the nucleus

  • Portfolio-design meeting with Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D.
  • Two two-page experiment briefs.
  • Introductions to Suzuki and Wang.
  • nano@stanford feasibility discussion.
  • Dry-lab requirements from actual apparatus.
Days 30–90

Freeze one campaign

  • Short consulting reviews where useful.
  • Freeze intervention, raw channels, fixtures, rules, and schema.
  • Separate RMC facility work from Stanford research.
  • Negotiate one SRA.
  • Install independent design review before first data.
After first result

Let the receipt decide

  • Null: preserve it and close the exact premise.
  • Positive: repeat with a second operator and apparatus.
  • Add advanced metrology only for the remaining unresolved nuisance.
  • Prototype only after independent repetition.
The first ask to Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D.

“Dr. Garry Nolan, Ph.D., we are not asking Stanford to endorse POAMS. We want to fund two tightly bounded experiments in which the intervention, raw observables, artifact injections, null conditions, and data custody are fixed before the run. We need your help identifying the best magnetic-material PI, device or sensor builder, and independent measurement critic—and choosing the correct Stanford mechanism for each role.”
