## 1. Taking POAMS's own definition seriously: candidate ratios, computed

**Definition:** α = (one fundamental mode) / (total AM configuration structure).

For this to be an *algorithm* rather than a slogan, "total configuration structure" must be a computable quantity. There are exactly three ways to make it one. I try each.

### Candidate 1: Total = cumulative mode capacity through shell N

α = 1ħ / Σₙ₌₁ᴺ 2n²

- N=5: 1/110. N=6: 1/182. N=7: 1/280.
- **No N gives 137.** Worse: N is a free truncation parameter (the sum diverges as N→∞), so even a hit would violate the forcing rule. The closest construction anyone could offer, α = 2ħ/280 = 1/140 (fundamental dipole over seven shells), misses by 2.2% — excluded at ~10⁷σ against CODATA precision. **Fail.**

### Candidate 2: Total = capacity-weighted structure including generators

Products of native integers: 2 × 6 × … — but here a hard theorem intervenes (see §3): **137 is prime.** No product of native integers greater than 1 can equal it. Every product-form candidate is dead on arrival. **Fail, provably.**

### Candidate 3: Total = a *measure* (volume) on the configuration space

If "total structure" is not a count but a continuous measure — say, the volume of the space of AM configurations, with the fundamental mode as a unit cell — then non-integer values like 137.036 become possible in principle.

But this is **exactly Wyler's move**. Wyler's α = (9/8π⁴)(π⁵/2⁴·5!)^¼ is a ratio of group-manifold volumes on SO(4,2)-related coset spaces. Gilmore (1972) showed why it fails: **volumes of non-compact group manifolds depend on arbitrary normalization conventions of the Haar measure.** Change the normalization, change the "derived" α. The number 137.03608 was reverse-engineered by convention choice, matches only ~6 digits, and is excluded at ~4000σ.

The devastating conclusion of Candidate 3: **POAMS's own definition of α, when pushed to full rigor, collapses back into the Wyler construction it is trying to escape.** A "fraction of total configuration structure" is either a count (Candidates 1–2, which fail on integers and parity) or a measure (Candidate 3, which reintroduces the convention-dependence that killed Wyler). ## 2. The wall, hit again — and now with a proof attached

The prior two-model analysis found: the mode structure gives **structure** (2n², 1/n², integer degeneracies) but not the **scale** 137.036. This attempt confirms it, and sharpens it into two structural obstructions:

**Obstruction 1 (arithmetic).** Representation theory of SO(3)/SO(3,1) — which is what the mode machinery *is* — outputs integers (dimensions, degeneracies, generator counts) and small rationals (coupling coefficients). α⁻¹ = 137.035999177 is, as far as anyone knows, neither. **Obstruction 2 (physical, and deeper).** α is a coupling *strength*, not a multiplicity. And critically: **α runs.** Measured at electron scale it is 1/137.036; at the Z mass it is ≈1/127.9. Any static counting of a fixed mode inventory produces one number with no scale dependence. A genuine POAMS derivation would have to explain not just 137.036 but *why that value holds at the atomic scale specifically and flows correctly elsewhere*. The mode-counting machinery contains no mechanism that could even express this. This is a category obstruction, not a near-miss.

## 3. Is "137" a counting result? Ruthless answer: no — and here is a small theorem

**Parity theorem (native, forced):** Every mode capacity is 2n² — even, because of the phase-sense factor of 2 that POAMS itself derives. Therefore **every sum of mode capacities is even. 137 is odd. No sum of POAMS mode capacities can equal 137.** This is the one rigorous α-adjacent result the structure forces, and it is a *no-go*.

Watch what happens when someone tries to evade it — this is worth exhibiting as a specimen of live numerology:

> "137 = 128 + 8 + 1 = (capacity of mode 8) + (capacity of mode 2) + (the one probe quantum)!"

Every defect on display: nothing forces modes 8 and 2 rather than any other subset summing to 136; the "+1" is an ad hoc unit appended precisely to defeat the parity theorem; and even granting all of it, the result is **137, not 137.035999** — wrong in the fourth digit, excluded at ~10⁶σ. This is Eddington's error re-enacted: he "derived" 136 from his E-number counting, then added 1 when experiment said 137, then experiment said 137.036 and the program died. POAMS must not walk that road.

The transcendental escape hatch is equally closed: 4π³ + π² + π = 137.036306… differs from 137.035999 in the sixth digit — **~15,000σ**, the same graveyard as Wyler. And the look-elsewhere problem is quantitative: with the constants {π, e, 2, 3, 5} and three or four operations, the space of expressible numbers is dense enough that six-digit coincidences with *any* target are expected by chance. A six-digit match selected after the fact carries approximately zero evidential weight. Twelve digits, predicted in advance, tracking CODATA updates — that is the bar, and nothing native clears it. Nothing native clears *four*.
